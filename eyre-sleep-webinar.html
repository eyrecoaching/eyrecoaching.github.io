The Problem

You're exhausted.
But you can't switch off.

You lie down and your mind starts. You fall asleep and wake at 2am. You're tired all day but wired at night. You've tried everything. Nothing sticks.

This isn't a sleep problem. It's a nervous system problem. Your body hasn't had the signal that the day is over. It's still running.

What It's Costing You

Poor sleep isn't just
tiredness.

Recovery
Your body can't repair
Growth hormone releases during deep sleep. No deep sleep — no recovery. Every training session, every hard day goes unprocessed.
2

3

You're running on debt
Cognitive performance, reaction time, emotional regulation — all degrade on poor sleep. You can push through. But you're borrowing from tomorrow.
Why It Happens

Your nervous system
didn't get the memo.

Your autonomic nervous system has two modes — sympathetic (on, activated, ready) and parasympathetic (off, recovered, safe). Sleep requires parasympathetic dominance.

Most people who can't sleep are stuck in sympathetic. Their body is physiologically prepared for threat, not rest. No amount of willpower overrides that signal.

The nervous system doesn't respond to "relax." It responds to physiological input — specifically, your breath.

Sympathetic — Stuck On
Heart rate elevated
Cortisol still circulating. Muscles slightly tensed. Mind scanning for problems. Body prepared for action — not sleep.
Parasympathetic — What You Need
Heart rate slowing
Digestion active. Muscles releasing. Mind quieting. Body temperature dropping. The nervous system has received a clear signal — the day is done.
The Mechanism

Your breath is the
fastest input you have.

The vagus nerve connects your brain stem to your heart, lungs, and gut. Every breath activates it. A slow nasal exhale sends a direct signal — parasympathetic, safe, downshift.

Mouth breathing keeps the system activated. Nasal breathing with an extended exhale begins the shift. This is not relaxation. This is physiology.

Nasal breathing also produces nitric oxide — a vasodilator that improves oxygen uptake and supports sleep quality. Mouth breathing produces none.

Tonight's Protocol

Simple. Not easy.
But simple.

Three changes. Start tonight. Build from here.

Don't try to do all of this perfectly. Pick one thing. Do it for seven days. Then add the next.

1
90 Minutes Before Bed
Stop the stimulation
No screens, no work, no difficult conversations. Dim the lights. Eat your last meal. Your nervous system needs a runway to land — give it one.
2
The Breathing Practice
Extended exhale — 4 in, 8 out
Nasal only. In for 4 counts. Out for 8 counts. Slow. Do this for 5 minutes lying down. The extended exhale activates the vagal brake. This is the signal your nervous system needs.
3
During Sleep
Nasal only
If you wake and find your mouth open, close it. Consider mouth tape — a small piece across the lips. Start with just the centre. It keeps nasal breathing consistent through the night.
