The Problem

You're exhausted.
But you can't switch off.

You lie down and your mind starts. You fall asleep and wake at 2am. You're tired all day but wired at night. You've tried everything. Nothing sticks.

This isn't a sleep problem. It's a nervous system problem. Your body hasn't had the signal that the day is over. It's still running.

Why It Happens

Your nervous system
didn't get the memo.

Your autonomic nervous system has two modes — sympathetic (on, activated, ready) and parasympathetic (off, recovered, safe). Sleep requires parasympathetic dominance.

Most people who can't sleep are stuck in sympathetic. Their body is physiologically prepared for threat, not rest. No amount of willpower overrides that signal.

The nervous system doesn't respond to "relax." It responds to physiological input — specifically, your breath.

Sympathetic — Stuck On
Heart rate elevated
Cortisol still circulating. Mind scanning for problems. Body prepared for action — not sleep.
Parasympathetic — What You Need
Heart rate slowing
Muscles releasing. Mind quieting. Body temperature dropping. The nervous system has received a clear signal — the day is done.

Mouth Taping

The simplest thing
most people won't try.

Most people mouth breathe during sleep without knowing it. A dry mouth in the morning is the tell. Mouth taping keeps lips closed during sleep — a gentle prompt, not a seal.

What to use
Surgical tape
Small piece. Centre of lips only to start. Nexcare or 3M surgical tape works. Build over a week.
How to build
One week
Night 1–2: wear for 20 mins before sleep, remove. Night 3–4: sleep with it. By night 7 it's normal.
What changes
Morning is different
Mouth moist not dry. Less snoring. HRV improves. You wake having actually recovered.

Not for everyone. If you have nasal obstruction or sleep apnoea — address those first.

Why Some People Can't Settle

CO2 tolerance.
Most people have never heard of it.

CO2 isn't just waste gas. It's the trigger that tells your body to breathe. Low CO2 tolerance means your nervous system fires the alarm earlier and more often — even during sleep.

People with low CO2 tolerance wake more easily, feel more anxious at night, and struggle to settle. It's not anxiety. It's physiology.

CO2 tolerance is trainable. The 30 Day Challenge includes the specific protocol. It takes weeks — but the difference is significant.

The 2am Wake

Why 2am?
It's not random.

Cortisol begins its natural rise around 2–4am to prepare the body for waking. In a regulated nervous system this is gradual and you sleep through it. In an activated system — it fires you awake.

When you wake at 2am — don't fight it. Slow nasal exhale. Four in, eight out. Three cycles. The system will downshift if you give it the signal.

What's happening
Cortisol spike
A regulated system absorbs it. An activated system treats it as a threat. You wake. Mind starts. Can't get back.
In the moment
4 in — 8 out
Nasal only. Don't reach for your phone. Three cycles. Most people are back asleep within minutes.
